Here is what shortens most water heaters' lives: corrosion from the inside and mineral buildup on the bottom, both of which are largely preventable. As water heats, dissolved minerals come out of the water and settle into a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On a gas unit, that layer insulates the water from the burner, so the steel runs hotter and the tank starts to rumble and pop. On an electric unit, it builds up around the lower element until the element burns out. Meanwhile the anode rod, a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not, is slowly used up. Once it is gone, the tank itself becomes the target. In La Mesa, CA, we see this pattern constantly, and most of it was preventable.

Here is what the visit covers. We drain the tank and flush out sediment until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. Warning Signs to Watch For

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • A tank that pops and knocks during a heating cycle
  • A tankless unit showing error codes or fluctuating temperatures
  • Hot water with a sulfur odor
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to
  • Small drips at the drain valve or connections
  • Hot water that looks cloudy or rust-colored
  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

A few maintenance tasks suit homeowners, and many do not. Keeping clutter away from the heater, checking for leaks, and noticing new noises are all useful. Flushing, anode replacement, relief valve testing, and burner or element work are a different matter. Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here's where to begin:

  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
  • Ask about the anode rod at each visit; it is the tank's main defense against rust
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often in hard water
