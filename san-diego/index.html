How San Diego Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year if their water is hard.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs like rusty hot water, rumbling during heating, dampness under the tank, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble needs only a flush, yet that same buildup, neglected for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts today so help is one call away as soon as something seems off. A quick call saves time, money, and ruined floors.
