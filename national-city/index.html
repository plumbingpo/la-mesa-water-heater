Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in National City

Many early failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or moisture around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a surprise cleanup.
