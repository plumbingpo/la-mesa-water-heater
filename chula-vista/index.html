Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Chula Vista

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year if their water is hard.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the manufacture date, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that point and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement once the tank fails.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement.
